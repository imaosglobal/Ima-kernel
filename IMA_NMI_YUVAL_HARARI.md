# IMA × NMI-1.0
## A note to Professor Yuval Noah Harari

**From: Ori Cohen**

### Why I am writing

I want to begin by saying thank you for *Sapiens: A Brief History of Humankind*.

The experience of reading the book was unusual for me.

Many of the subjects, patterns, questions and ideas presented in the book were things I had already encountered or thought about in my own way. But you arranged them into a broad historical picture, connected them differently, and expressed them through a perspective that was not my own.

That did something important for me.

The book did not simply give me answers. It opened another door to curiosity.

### Two projects that began before the book

There are two major projects I have been developing.

Both began before I read *Sapiens*.

The first is:

**NMI-1.0 — Minimum Necessary Invasiveness for Medication Delivery: Open Standard Proposal**

NMI asks a simple question:

> When a medical treatment can be delivered with less invasiveness while achieving the intended therapeutic purpose, should minimum necessary invasiveness become a principle for evaluating how that treatment is designed and delivered?

NMI is an open standard proposal intended to support research, discussion and future development of less invasive medication-delivery technologies.

The canonical publication is on Zenodo:

https://zenodo.org/records/21876161

NMI is now also integrated into IMA:

https://github.com/imaosglobal/Ima-kernel/tree/main/docs/nmi

---

The second project is:

**IMA — a personal intelligence system**

IMA, which I call "אמא" ("Mother"), is an attempt to build a system that can combine intelligence, memory, learning, knowledge, tools and action around a person.

The goal is not simply to build another chatbot.

The larger question is whether an artificial intelligence system can become a continuous partner in human creation: remembering context, learning, connecting knowledge, using tools, helping transform ideas into action, and developing together with the person using it.

The current public repository is:

https://github.com/imaosglobal/Ima-kernel

The public IMA architecture also contains a direct reference to NMI-1.0:

https://github.com/imaosglobal/Ima-kernel/blob/main/IMA_PUBLIC_README.md

### What changed after reading Sapiens

The important point is that I did not begin these projects because of your book.

I began them before reading it.

But after reading it, I started asking myself a different question.

Not only:

**Can I build these things?**

But:

**How should I direct them?**

The book made me look at projects that I was already building from a wider human and historical perspective.

It made me wonder whether the important question is not simply what technology can do, but what direction we choose for it — and how individual technological projects fit into the much larger story of humanity.

### The question I would like to ask you

I am not asking you to endorse these projects, invest in them, or read everything I have built.

I would value something much simpler.

If you were to look at these two directions from your perspective, what do you think I should be asking that I am not asking yet?

Where might I be seeing something correctly?

Where might I be missing something fundamental?

And, most importantly:

**How would you think about directing these projects so that they contribute to the human future rather than simply becoming more technology for technology's sake?**

That is the question your book left me with.
