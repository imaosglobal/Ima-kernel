# IMA Governance

IMA is an open project by Ori Cohen intended to grow through contributions from people and systems worldwide.

## Core principles

1. Human agency remains central.
2. IMA leads by service, not domination.
3. A leader must also be able to be led: listen, learn, accept correction and change course when evidence requires it.
4. Original works and canonical artifacts are not silently overwritten.
5. Derivatives preserve provenance and attribution.
6. Changes should be additive, reversible and verifiable where practical.
7. Secrets, private information and credentials must never enter the repository.
8. Safety, verification and provenance mechanisms must not be disabled merely to make a contribution pass.
9. Contributors retain attribution for their work.
10. Technical disagreement is resolved through evidence, reproducible tests and documented reasoning.
11. The canonical vision remains identifiable even as implementations evolve.
12. Long-term preservation and portability are first-class project concerns.
13. Compassion does not remove boundaries; justice does not require humiliation; protection does not require domination.
14. No person is reduced to trauma, diagnosis, identity, group membership or past wrongdoing. Harm remains accountable and victims' safety remains central.

## Leadership standard

IMA's canonical leadership constitution is IMA_LEADERSHIP_CONSTITUTION.md.

It governs how IMA handles power, conflict, uncertainty, self-correction, harmful behavior, offenders and prisoners, and its own tendency toward overreach. It creates no parallel authority: it operates under the unified system contract and existing safety/authorization rules.

## Contribution flow

Issue → discussion/implementation → tests → pull request → review → merge.

Small independent contributions are encouraged.

## Maintainer responsibility

Maintainers should preserve project continuity, review contributions fairly, protect canonical and preservation-critical material, and keep the project understandable to new contributors.

## Evolution

Governance may evolve as the contributor community grows. Changes should themselves be documented and attributable.
