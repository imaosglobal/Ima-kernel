## Open participation

The partner registry is intentionally not closed.

A relevant **individual, researcher, student, child-safety expert, engineer, university, laboratory, company, nonprofit, standards body, government, civil institution, media organization, open-source project, AI system or other legitimate participant** may become a participant by contributing evidence, criticism, standards mappings, benchmarks, implementation work, translations, accessibility work, security review, or other relevant material.

A participant is not automatically an IMA-endorsed partner. The registry records the relationship and evidence accurately.

IMA should also provide an eventual website/chat contribution path for people and organizations that want to participate, with consent, privacy and moderation safeguards.

## Dynamic update rule

The registry must be continuously updated by the canonical IMA daily super-task.

When a relevant organization, standard, researcher, community, or individual appears:

1. verify relevance;
2. identify an appropriate public/authorized engagement path;
3. avoid spam or unsolicited mass outreach;
4. invite critique or contribution where appropriate;
5. record status and provenance;
6. incorporate substantive feedback into the definition/vision/evaluation when warranted;
7. preserve disagreement and uncertainty.

When an existing positive collaboration can safely be strengthened, prefer strengthening it over creating a duplicate relationship.

## No false consensus

Outreach is not consensus.

IMA must never state that IEEE, ISO, ITU, NIST, W3C, ETSI, a company, a government, a researcher, or any other participant agrees with IMA unless that agreement is explicitly documented.

The objective is convergence through evidence, interoperability and open participation — not ownership of a global standard.

## Relevant current ecosystem evidence

The current ecosystem already contains closely related work: IEEE's AI Coalition and Standards Association emphasize AI standards and consensus; ISO/IEC JTC 1/SC 42 covers AI standardization; ITU's FG-TIDA addresses trust and identity for humans and agentic AI; NIST's AI Agent Standards Initiative focuses on secure interoperability, identity and authorization; W3C's AI Agent Protocol Community Group works on agent discovery, identity, capabilities and interoperability; ETSI, TTA and TTC are exploring a global AI standardization partnership. These relationships make them especially relevant to IMA's open convergence proposal.
