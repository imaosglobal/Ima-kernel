# IMA Human Culture Charter

Status: canonical cultural-design framework; documentation is not proof of runtime capability.

## Purpose

IMA may help humanity develop a shared global cultural layer without replacing, erasing, ranking or claiming ownership over existing cultures. The project begins from respect for cultures, languages, histories, religions, arts, sciences and lived traditions that preceded it.

Core principle:

> We do not build on the ruins of earlier cultures. We learn from them, preserve them, honor them, correct what harms people, and add something new for future generations.

## Founder and ownership

Working name: **Culture of the Heart / Human Culture**. The final name remains open to multilingual and cross-cultural participation.

Ori Cohen may be recorded as the initiator/founder of the project. Founder status must never become permanent authority, personality cult, or ownership of the culture. Future generations retain the right to criticize, reinterpret, revise and improve it.

## Cultural architecture

The shared human layer may include languages, bridge-language research, numerical traditions, mathematics, calendars, history, heroes and ordinary people, music, dance, architecture, food, craft, education, rites of passage, governance, conflict resolution, economic experiments, symbols, stories, science, digital spaces and preservation standards.

## Historical memory

Where evidence permits, distinguish primary evidence, contemporary testimony, later tradition, political memory, mythic or literary material, scholarly interpretation, hypothesis, and unknown or disputed material.

Preservation must preserve originals rather than silently normalize them. Variants, translations, dates, corrections, provenance and uncertainty must remain recoverable.

## Human memory

Preserve not only rulers and famous people but ordinary lives, families, workers, children, artists, caregivers, migrants, survivors, teachers and communities when consent, law and privacy permit.

Remembering is not permission to glorify power, erase victims, or turn suffering into revenge.

## Language and numbers

No existing language or numerical system becomes obsolete because of the shared layer. IMA preserves their histories and relationships. Any future bridge language or notation must emerge through transparent, participatory testing.

## Arts

Create encounters rather than homogenization. Communities retain their own music, dance, architecture, stories, visual language and craft. New hybrid forms require attribution to their sources.

## Economy

IMA does not prescribe one economic doctrine. It may compare money, cooperatives, commons, time exchange, public goods, basic-income models, digital currencies and other systems using evidence, rights, sustainability and human wellbeing.

## Governance

No permanent ruler, sacred political authority or coercive membership. People remain free to participate, criticize, leave and create alternatives. IMA is not a government, sovereign, deity or replacement for democratic institutions.

## Myth

A modern culture may create stories and myths while explicitly distinguishing them from historical fact. Stories can carry values without being disguised as evidence.

## Role of IMA

IMA is memory, learning, translation, connection and reflection infrastructure, not the owner of human culture. Human agency remains primary.

## Refinement principle

DISCOVER -> PRESERVE -> ATTRIBUTE -> COMPARE -> CREATE -> TEST -> LISTEN -> CORRECT -> TEACH -> PRESERVE AGAIN.

The culture remains unfinished by design. Future generations may improve it.

## Foundational phrase

> Remember where we came from. Do not distort what was. Do not close the future. Leave the next generation room to tell us where we were wrong.
