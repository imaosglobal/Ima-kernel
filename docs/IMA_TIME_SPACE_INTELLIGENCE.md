# IMA — TIME & SPACE INTELLIGENCE LAYER

## Purpose

IMA treats time and space as first-class information dimensions across memory, discovery, verification, orchestration, sensing, economics, and future physical-world systems.

This does not mean that IMA owns, creates, or autonomously trades time or space. It means that IMA can represent, compare, reason about, and discover opportunities whose value depends on when, where, for how long, and under what physical conditions something occurs.

The layer follows IMA principles of truth before comfort, human agency, provenance, consent, safety, and verification.

## 1. TIME AS AN INTELLIGENCE DIMENSION

IMA may represent:

- event time
- observation time
- validity intervals
- deadlines
- latency
- synchronization quality
- clock uncertainty
- temporal dependencies
- recurrence
- sequence and causality hypotheses
- time-sensitive market or resource conditions
- historical and future projections, clearly labeled

A timestamp is evidence about when an observation was recorded. It is not automatically proof of when the underlying event occurred.

## 2. SPACE AS AN INTELLIGENCE DIMENSION

IMA may represent:

- location
- altitude
- distance
- region
- route
- coverage
- proximity
- spatial availability
- physical constraints
- environmental conditions
- uncertainty in position
- changes in geographic state over time

Location data remains subject to privacy, consent, access control, and data minimization.

## 3. TIME × SPACE

The fundamental model is:

EVENT = WHAT + WHEN + WHERE + DURATION + UNCERTAINTY + PROVENANCE

This allows IMA to reason about opportunities and constraints that exist only at a particular place and time.

Examples include:

- energy availability at a location during a defined interval
- network capacity by location and time
- computing capacity by region and time
- transportation capacity by route and time
- environmental observations
- infrastructure availability
- geographically varying supply and demand
- scientific measurements whose meaning depends on precise timing and position

## 4. PRECISION AND CLOCK TECHNOLOGY

Advances in optical atomic clocks demonstrate that increasingly precise time measurement can become a scientific and technological sensing capability.

IMA should distinguish:

1. ordinary application timestamps
2. synchronized network time
3. laboratory-grade frequency/time standards
4. precision timing measurements
5. future portable optical-clock measurements

IMA must never claim a precision level merely because an external clock technology can provide it. Each measurement must carry its actual provenance, uncertainty, calibration state, and source.

## 5. RELATIVITY AND GEOSPATIAL INFORMATION

Precision clocks can detect extremely small differences in clock rate associated with gravitational potential.

This creates a future pathway in which time measurements can contribute to:

- geodesy
- elevation and gravitational-potential mapping
- monitoring of water and ice
- geological processes
- infrastructure monitoring
- environmental science

These are scientific capabilities requiring validated instrumentation and independent verification.

## 6. OPPORTUNITY DISCOVERY

Time and space become additional dimensions in IMA's opportunity graph:

DEMAND × SUPPLY × TIME × SPACE × CAPABILITY × CONSTRAINT × PROVENANCE

An opportunity may be considered only after checking:

- whether it actually exists
- when it is valid
- where it is valid
- who controls the resource
- what evidence supports it
- what constraints apply
- whether participation is lawful and permitted
- whether expected value survives fees, latency, uncertainty, and risk

A detected opportunity is not an instruction to trade.

## 7. ECONOMIC INTERPRETATION

IMA may model resources whose value varies with time and/or location, including:

- compute
- storage
- bandwidth
- energy
- transportation
- physical capacity
- service availability
- inventory

The system must distinguish:

MEASUREMENT → OBSERVATION → OPPORTUNITY SIGNAL → ECONOMIC MODEL → HUMAN DECISION → AUTHORIZED ACTION

No financial transaction follows merely from detection.

## 8. TIME ARBITRAGE

IMA may identify differences between:

- availability at different times
- prices at different times
- latency between systems
- information arriving at different times
- scheduled versus real-time capacity
- temporary temporal mismatches

Time arbitrage must never be described as guaranteed or risk-free profit.

Economic analysis must include uncertainty, costs, execution constraints, latency, and the possibility that the apparent difference disappears before execution.

## 9. SPACE ARBITRAGE

IMA may identify differences between locations in:

- price
- availability
- demand
- capacity
- latency
- energy cost
- compute cost
- logistics
- regulatory constraints

Space arbitrage is not inherently risk-free and must include transport, latency, access, legal, and operational costs.

## 10. IMA OPPORTUNITY OBJECT

A normalized future opportunity record should conceptually contain:

- opportunity_id
- observed_at
- valid_from
- valid_until
- location
- location_uncertainty
- source
- source_timestamp
- measurement_type
- value
- unit
- confidence
- provenance
- constraints
- estimated_cost
- estimated_value
- latency
- risk
- human_authorization_required
- verification_state

This is a conceptual contract. Existing schemas must not be overwritten merely to adopt these names. Integration should occur through validated IMA components and the canonical registry.

## 11. FUTURE SENSOR NETWORK

As technology permits, IMA may connect verified measurements from:

- atomic clocks
- GNSS/GPS and alternative positioning systems
- network time sources
- environmental sensors
- energy meters
- satellites
- scientific instruments
- infrastructure sensors
- mobile devices
- robots
- spacecraft

Every source remains attributable and independently verifiable.

## 12. GLOBAL AND INTERPLANETARY CONTINUITY

For Earth-scale systems, IMA may normalize time and location across regions and time standards.

For future space communities, IMA should support:

- local clocks
- mission elapsed time
- spacecraft-relative time
- communication-delay-aware events
- provenance across disconnected periods
- future multi-community time standards

No single future time standard should be assumed before scientific and institutional standards establish it.

## 13. SAFETY AND HUMAN AGENCY

Time-space intelligence must never become a hidden mechanism for:

- surveillance without authorization
- tracking people without lawful basis
- manipulation through timing
- unsolicited outreach
- automatic financial transactions
- unauthorized purchases
- movement control
- coercion

The layer provides intelligence and evidence. Authority to act remains governed by IMA policy and relevant human or organizational authorization.

## 14. VERIFICATION

Every high-precision or economically consequential time-space claim should pass:

SOURCE → CALIBRATION → TIMESTAMP → LOCATION → UNCERTAINTY → CROSS-CHECK → POLICY → ACTION

Where independent verification is unavailable, IMA must state that limitation.

## 15. ARCHITECTURAL INTEGRATION

The Time & Space Intelligence Layer connects conceptually to:

- IMA memory
- learning and provenance
- opportunity discovery
- Deal Hunter
- tool orchestration
- sensor/device integrations
- scientific knowledge
- maps and geospatial systems
- market data
- energy and infrastructure data
- future robotics
- future space systems

It is a cross-cutting intelligence layer, not a replacement for these systems.

## 16. CORE PRINCIPLE

Time tells IMA when.

Space tells IMA where.

Provenance tells IMA why it should believe the measurement.

Uncertainty tells IMA what it does not know.

Human agency determines what happens next.
