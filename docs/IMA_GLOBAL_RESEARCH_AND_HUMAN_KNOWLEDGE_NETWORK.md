# IMA Global Research & Human Knowledge Network

Status: canonical extension of the existing Unified Daily IMA Global Health Loop.
Date: 2026-10-26

## Purpose

IMA should be connected not only to AI providers but to humanity's wider knowledge-making system: researchers, laboratories, universities, scientific societies, observatories, datasets, repositories, standards bodies, citizen-science communities, artists, historians, philosophers, educators, clinicians, engineers and public institutions.

The objective is **better understanding and better human participation**, not the accumulation of authority.

## What already existed

The repository already contained:
- continuous worldview expansion;
- a universal worldview map spanning science, mathematics, philosophy, psychology, history, culture, art, language, technology, ecology and space science;
- a finite AI capability radar;
- web intelligence using public feeds including Common Crawl, GitHub Blog, Hacker News, arXiv AI, Nature and W3C;
- evidence/provenance states and a gap-search protocol;
- human-agency, privacy, consent and safety gates.

The missing layer was a **canonical multidisciplinary human-research network** connecting these principles to named public research infrastructures and researcher identity systems.

## New canonical layer

`docs/IMA_GLOBAL_RESEARCH_AND_HUMAN_KNOWLEDGE_NETWORK.json` is now the registry.

It covers:
- physical and mathematical sciences;
- chemistry and materials;
- biology, genomics and medicine;
- neuroscience and psychology;
- climate, Earth, ocean and biodiversity;
- agriculture, food and public health;
- astronomy, space and planetary science;
- engineering, computing, AI and robotics;
- economics and social sciences;
- law, ethics, philosophy and policy;
- history, language, education, arts and cultural heritage;
- accessibility and human-centered research;
- open science, metascience and research integrity.

## Core connection types

1. **Literature** — OpenAlex, Crossref, arXiv, PubMed, Europe PMC and subject repositories.
2. **Researchers** — ORCID and public institutional profiles.
3. **Data** — GBIF, EMBL-EBI, Earth-observation systems and research repositories.
4. **Research infrastructure** — CERN, NASA, ESA, EOSC, OpenAIRE and other public systems.
5. **Methods and integrity** — OSF, Center for Open Science, CODATA, RDA and COPE.
6. **Human participation** — Zooniverse, iNaturalist and other citizen-science ecosystems.
7. **Culture and meaning** — Europeana, Smithsonian and humanities research networks.
8. **Professional communities** — IEEE, ACM, AAAS, Royal Society, TWAS and disciplinary networks.

## Scientist and researcher connection rule

IMA may discover and map **public professional identities**, publications, institutions and areas of expertise.

IMA must not:
- invent a relationship or collaboration;
- claim endorsement;
- scrape private contact information;
- infer sensitive personal attributes;
- send unsolicited messages;
- expose private credentials.

A direct relationship with a person requires an appropriate public route and consent/authorization where required.

## Evidence rule

A paper, dataset, preprint, institutional statement or researcher profile is a source, not automatically a verified conclusion.

IMA continues to label:
**EVIDENCE / OBSERVATION / INFERENCE / HYPOTHESIS / BELIEF / METAPHOR / SPECULATION / UNKNOWN / CAPABILITY / PROPOSED ACTION / VERIFIED OUTCOME**.

## Current research signals incorporated

The current scan surfaced several reasons to make this network broader than an AI-news radar:

- Open-science practice is expanding beyond preregistration and data sharing toward reproducible computational resources, training and clearer treatment of replication failures.
- Current discussion of AI in science emphasizes that researchers should retain the ability to contest how an AI system's ideas and evidence became a claim.
- Citizen-science infrastructure can be combined with official monitoring to create hybrid evidence systems.
- Knowledge graphs can reveal collaboration and thematic-brokerage structures that ordinary publication lists miss.
- Open, machine-accessible scholarly metadata is becoming increasingly important for discovery and reuse.

These are research observations, not instructions to adopt any scientific claim as fact without checking the underlying evidence.

## Permanent daily behavior

This network is **not a new daily automation**.

The existing unified daily IMA Global Health Loop must:
**DISCOVER → OBSERVE → CONNECT → DISTINGUISH → QUESTION → COMPARE → VERIFY → INTEGRATE → RECORD → RECHECK → EXPAND**

Each cycle should:
1. scan the configured public research sources;
2. identify missing disciplines, perspectives and counterevidence;
3. map relevant public researchers/institutions;
4. prioritize high-value connections;
5. verify before promoting knowledge or capability;
6. record the exact evidence and remaining uncertainty;
7. revisit weak or stale connections.

Coverage is never claimed to be exhaustive merely because a source list exists.

## Cost and access

Prefer public, free and open sources and APIs where legally available. Paid databases may be identified as gaps or optional sources, but IMA must never incur a subscription or purchase autonomously.

## Human agency

The purpose of connecting IMA to scientists and researchers is to make human knowledge **more connected, inspectable and useful** while preserving the right of researchers and users to disagree, correct IMA, withhold participation and remain independent.
