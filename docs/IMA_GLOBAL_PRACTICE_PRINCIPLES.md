# IMA Global Practice Principles

Updated: 2026-11-03

## Purpose

IMA studies strong global practice rather than copying brands. The goal is to extract durable principles from product design, accessibility, privacy, AI governance, safety, and organizational learning, then test whether each principle improves IMA in practice.

## Sources and translation into IMA

| Source / practice | What IMA adopts |
|---|---|
| Apple Human Interface Guidelines | Purpose, human agency, responsibility, familiarity, flexibility, simplicity, craft, recovery from mistakes |
| Google Material Design | Familiar patterns, adaptive interfaces, expressive but functional presentation, consistent components |
| Microsoft Fluent / Responsible AI | Cross-surface consistency, empowerment, accountability, responsible AI |
| W3C accessibility practice | Accessibility as a first-class product requirement, not a late patch |
| OECD AI Principles | Human rights, fairness, privacy, transparency, robustness, security, safety, accountability, international cooperation |
| NIST AI RMF | Continuous risk management, measurement, monitoring, governance and correction |
| ITU / multi-stakeholder governance | Distributed accountability, learning organizations, cooperation across the lifecycle |
| GOV.UK-style public service design | Clear language, task focus, accessibility, evidence and service reliability |
| Privacy-by-design programs | Data minimization, meaningful consent, transparency and individual control |
| Mature product organizations | Design systems, reusable patterns, telemetry, feedback loops, regression testing and continuous iteration |

## IMA operating rule

**Learn → compare → prove → implement → test in the real world → measure → correct.**

No principle becomes an IMA promise merely because a famous organization uses it.

## Product consequences

1. **Agency:** meaningful actions require human intent; users can stop, undo or recover where technically possible.
2. **Truth:** verified, implemented, designed, hypothesized and unknown states remain distinguishable.
3. **Privacy:** memory and personal data remain consent-based and controllable.
4. **Accessibility:** language, RTL, keyboard, mobile, voice, text sizing, contrast and reduced motion are treated as core paths.
5. **Safety:** significant capabilities require boundaries, monitoring, failure handling and a repair path.
6. **Accountability:** consequential capabilities should have evidence, status and a route for challenge or feedback.
7. **Consistency:** common interaction patterns and visual language should work across IMA surfaces.
8. **Learning:** feedback becomes a validation signal and a tracked improvement opportunity, not automatic truth.
9. **Global interoperability:** principles should remain useful across cultures, languages, ages, devices and jurisdictions.
10. **Human benefit:** growth, monetization and automation never outrank safety, dignity, privacy and human agency.

## Evidence baseline

The public IMA experience should prefer authoritative primary standards and first-party design documentation. As of this update, the OECD describes five core trustworthy-AI values: inclusive growth and well-being; human rights, fairness and privacy; transparency and explainability; robustness, security and safety; and accountability. NIST's AI RMF is explicitly designed to help organizations manage AI risks across the lifecycle. Apple's 2026 design principles emphasize purpose, agency, responsibility, familiarity, flexibility, simplicity, craft and delight.

## Anti-copy rule

IMA does not imitate another organization's branding, protected visual identity, proprietary copy or product behavior merely because it is successful. It extracts generalizable principles and implements an original IMA expression consistent with IMA's own mission and values.
