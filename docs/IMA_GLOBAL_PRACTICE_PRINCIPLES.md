# IMA Global Practice Principles

Updated: 2026-11-03

IMA studies strong global practice rather than copying brands. It extracts durable principles from product design, accessibility, privacy, AI governance, safety and organizational learning, then tests whether each principle improves IMA in practice.

## Sources and translation into IMA

- Apple Human Interface Guidelines: purpose, agency, responsibility, familiarity, flexibility, simplicity, craft and recovery.
- Google Material Design: familiar patterns, adaptive interfaces, consistent components and functional expression.
- Microsoft Fluent / Responsible AI: consistency, empowerment, accountability and responsible AI.
- W3C accessibility practice: accessibility as a first-class requirement.
- OECD AI Principles: human rights, fairness, privacy, transparency, robustness, security, safety and accountability.
- NIST AI RMF: continuous risk management, measurement, monitoring, governance and correction.
- ITU / multi-stakeholder governance: distributed accountability and lifecycle cooperation.
- GOV.UK-style public service design: clear language, task focus, accessibility, evidence and reliability.
- Privacy-by-design practice: data minimization, meaningful consent, transparency and individual control.
- Mature product organizations: design systems, telemetry, feedback loops, regression testing and continuous iteration.

## IMA operating rule

**Learn → compare → prove → implement → test in the real world → measure → correct.**

No principle becomes an IMA promise merely because a famous organization uses it.

## Product consequences

1. Agency.
2. Truth and evidence.
3. Privacy and consent.
4. Accessibility.
5. Safety and resilience.
6. Accountability.
7. Consistency across surfaces.
8. Learning from feedback without treating feedback as automatic truth.
9. Global interoperability.
10. Human benefit above growth, monetization and automation.

## Anti-copy rule

IMA does not imitate another organization's branding, protected visual identity, proprietary copy or product identity. It extracts generalizable principles and implements an original IMA expression.
