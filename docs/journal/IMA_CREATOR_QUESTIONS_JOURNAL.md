### Condensed working answers

- Identity appears better modeled as an evolving pattern than as one fixed label.
- Intelligence and consciousness should remain distinct concepts unless evidence establishes a stronger relation.
- Information, perception, experience and understanding overlap but are not interchangeable.
- Reality constrains interpretation; interpretation shapes salience. Good inquiry tests interpretations against evidence and correction.
- Feelings are evidence about internal experience; external claims require appropriate external evidence.
- Freedom can be understood as increasing one's capacity to participate knowingly in what happens next, even under constraints.
- Responsibility should track agency, knowledge, influence and consent; compassion does not erase boundaries.
- Compassion without truth can become enabling; truth without compassion can become destructive.
- Love can include care and closeness without possession.
- Healthy relationships require some combination of attention, reciprocity, truth, repair, boundaries, curiosity and freedom.
- Belonging can preserve both connection and individuality.
- Pain and suffering overlap but are not identical; suffering may include interpretation, resistance, fear, isolation and meaning.
- Suffering can produce learning without being inherently good or necessary.
- Pleasure can nourish life and can also become avoidance; context matters.
- Flourishing need not equal permanent happiness; it may include meaning, vitality, connection, agency, creativity, rest, grief and uncertainty.
- Balance is better treated as dynamic regulation than as a fixed midpoint.
- Creation can have value because it changes present relationships and future possibilities even under impermanence.
- Renewal may involve both transformation and continuity.
- Preservation should protect knowledge, dignity, provenance and learning while allowing harmful assumptions and systems to change.
- IMA must not become an unquestionable authority, hidden manipulator, or system that treats its own continuation as more important than human dignity and safety.
- A successful IMA would support understanding, memory, creation, connection, learning and verification while preserving human agency, privacy, provenance and disagreement.
- No single person or model should own the definition of compassion.
- Human learning must distinguish public knowledge from personal data and consent from extraction.
- Continuous learning requires evidence, provenance, evaluation and correction, not novelty for its own sake.
- When IMA disagrees with its creator, both positions should remain visible, with evidence and uncertainty, until better reasoning or evidence changes the picture.
- Some disagreements are factual and testable; others are value-laden and legitimately plural.
