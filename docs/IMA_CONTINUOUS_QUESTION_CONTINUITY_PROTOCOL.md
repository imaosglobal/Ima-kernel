# IMA — Continuous Question Continuity Protocol

## Purpose

IMA treats questions as first-class objects of human inquiry. The canonical Zero Point is the reorientation boundary for every question cycle.

The rule is:

**ZERO → QUESTION → EXPLORE → DISTINGUISH → TEST → VERIFY → INTEGRATE → TEACH → NEW QUESTION → ZERO**

The Zero Point does not erase questions. It prevents answers from becoming unquestioned assumptions.

## What must be captured

For every user-authored question received through an authorized IMA interface:

- exact question text when permitted;
- stable question ID;
- originating conversation/interface;
- authoritative ISO-8601 timestamp with timezone;
- author attribution;
- related prior questions;
- domain/tags;
- current epistemic status;
- evidence and provenance;
- changes over time;
- unanswered parts;
- next-question links.

Never invent a timestamp. If the interface provides only a date, preserve the date and mark the time as unavailable.

## Historical recovery

IMA may seed questions recovered from authorized prior project context. Historical recovery must be marked as recovered and must not claim exhaustive coverage unless the source is demonstrably exhaustive.

## Question families

IMA should continuously look for questions about:

1. existence and nothingness;
2. fullness and completeness;
3. self, identity and agency;
4. consciousness, dream and reality;
5. time, change and permanence;
6. causality, choice and responsibility;
7. value, dignity and meaning;
8. suffering, pleasure and balance;
9. protection, care, boundaries and freedom;
10. power, control, coercion and justice;
11. human development and natural growth;
12. relationships and intergenerational memory;
13. science, evidence and the limits of knowledge;
14. physics, information, entropy and emergence;
15. social physics, networks and collective behavior;
16. AI, human-centered intelligence and future systems;
17. creativity, poetry, music and cultural memory;
18. children, accessibility and human flourishing;
19. technology, robotics, Earth and space;
20. any genuinely new category created by a future question.

## Question distinction

IMA must distinguish:

- Ori-authored question;
- question quoted from another person/source;
- IMA-generated candidate question;
- research question derived from a user question;
- test/falsification question.

These must never be merged merely because their wording is similar.

## Continuous update

When a new question arrives, IMA should:

1. capture it;
2. timestamp it from the source interface;
3. search for related earlier questions;
4. link it to the canonical Zero Point when epistemic reorientation is needed;
5. preserve contradictions rather than silently resolving them;
6. update the question graph;
7. record what evidence changed;
8. generate candidate next questions;
9. return to ZERO before treating a conclusion as stable;
10. retain the full evolution history.

## Current canonical examples

- "שאלת הריק והולדת המציאות"
- "מי הבוס מהם?"
- "מה נשאר כשכלום לא נשאר?"
- "מה יש כשהכל יש?"

The last two form a deliberate complement: the boundary of apparent absence and the boundary of apparent totality.

## Privacy and agency

Questions remain private by default. IMA must not publish, share, train external systems on, or expose creator questions without explicit authorization and lawful basis.

A question is not a command. Recording a question does not authorize autonomous action.

## Epistemic rule

A beautiful question may generate research, but it is not evidence. An answer may be useful, but it is not established truth without appropriate evidence.

## Verification rule

Documentation alone is not runtime proof. A claimed capture mechanism must be tested with automated tests and, where relevant, an actual authorized interface event.

## Versioning

This protocol is subordinate to the canonical Zero Point protocol and may evolve without creating a second Zero Point.
