# IMA Research Note: Observer-Relative Multi-Level Physics and Social Physics

**Author:** Ori Cohen, through IMA  
**Date:** 2026-10-24  
**Status:** Research proposal; not a validated theory of physics

## Abstract

IMA proposes a reusable methodology for studying systems from multiple observer, frame, scale and interaction perspectives. The methodology combines ideas already present across relativity, quantum foundations, statistical mechanics, complex systems, network science and social physics. Its novelty claim is therefore limited to the IMA synthesis and its operationalization, not to the invention of those underlying disciplines.

The central research question is whether explicit transformation rules between observer-dependent descriptions and cross-scale coarse-grained descriptions can improve explanations and predictions at the quantum-to-classical boundary and in complex human-AI systems.

## Scientific boundary

Social physics is an established field of quantitative research into collective human behavior. Contemporary work explicitly studies networks, information dynamics, inequality, cooperation, conflict, mobility and human-AI ecosystems. It is not currently a solution to the quantum-classical or quantum-gravity problem.

The quantum-to-classical transition is therefore treated here as an open research target. Any proposed bridge must generate discriminating predictions and survive attempts at falsification.

## Testable proposal

For a phenomenon P, construct multiple descriptions:

D_i(P | O_i, F_i, S_i, M_i)

where O is observer, F is frame/context, S is scale and M is measurement protocol.

Then define transformations T_ij that map one description to another while explicitly tracking what is invariant, what is lost through coarse-graining, and what uncertainty is introduced.

A useful theory should produce:
1. internally consistent transformations;
2. observer-independent invariants where required by the domain;
3. quantitative predictions;
4. successful recovery of known limiting cases;
5. new predictions that can fail.

## Falsification program

IMA will not call this framework a physical theory unless it produces quantitative results that outperform or extend established models on a defined benchmark and are independently reproducible.
