# Pleasure, Suffering, and Balance — Ori Cohen

**Creator:** Ori Cohen (אורי כהן)
**Date of first formulation:** 2026-09-19
**Status:** Original research question and evolving philosophical framework
**Project:** IMA — אמא

## Core question

If Buddhist philosophy developed a deep investigation of suffering, craving, attachment, and liberation from suffering, can there also be a systematic investigation of pleasure, joy, vitality, love, play, creativity, meaning, and human flourishing?

And beyond the two poles:

**What insights best balance suffering and pleasure?**

## Three-part inquiry

### I. The inquiry of suffering

A major Buddhist framework asks about suffering, its conditions, its cessation, and a path of practice.

This project does not attempt to replace or redefine Buddhist teaching. It asks what becomes visible when the direction of inquiry is deliberately turned toward the positive dimensions of human experience.

### II. The inquiry of pleasure

A preliminary four-part structure:

1. **Pleasure exists.**
   Pleasure includes more than sensory gratification: joy, beauty, love, curiosity, play, music, creativity, discovery, connection, and meaningful engagement.

2. **Pleasure has conditions.**
   Security, freedom, connection, embodiment, attention, competence, novelty, love, play, and meaning can all contribute to positive experience.

3. **Pleasures differ.**
   Immediate pleasantness and long-term human flourishing are not necessarily identical. A central research task is to distinguish forms of pleasure by their effects, durability, relational consequences, and relationship to freedom and agency.

4. **The capacity for pleasure can itself be cultivated.**
   The question is not only how to obtain pleasant experiences, but how a human being can become more capable of recognizing, receiving, creating, sharing, and sustaining what is good.

### III. The inquiry of balance

The deepest question may not be whether suffering or pleasure is preferable.

It may be:

> How can a human being learn to experience both suffering and pleasure without being consumed by either?

Possible balancing pairs:

- suffering ↔ pleasure
- avoidance ↔ participation
- release ↔ creation
- acceptance ↔ desire
- stillness ↔ enthusiasm
- non-attachment ↔ love
- awareness ↔ experience
- meaning ↔ enjoyment

The hypothesis is that mature flourishing may involve neither the elimination of desire nor the unlimited pursuit of pleasure, but a capacity for conscious participation in life.

## Intellectual lineage to investigate

This framework should be studied against prior work rather than presented as historically unprecedented.

Relevant traditions include:

- Buddhism and its analyses of suffering, craving, attachment, and liberation
- Epicurean philosophy and its analysis of pleasure, pain, desire, tranquility, and the good life
- Aristotle and eudaimonia
- utilitarian and hedonist traditions
- modern philosophy of well-being
- positive psychology and the distinction between hedonic and eudaimonic well-being
- contemporary research on pleasure, reward, motivation, meaning, connection, and flourishing

## Originality statement

The individual themes above have substantial precedents.

The potentially original contribution of this project is the **specific synthesis and research program**: placing a systematic inquiry of pleasure alongside the inquiry of suffering, then investigating a third domain of balancing insights between the two.

This is a research claim, not a claim that no one in history has ever asked a similar question.

## Research question in one sentence

**If humanity has spent millennia learning how to understand suffering, can we investigate pleasure with equal depth—and then discover the principles that allow a human life to hold both without being ruled by either?**
