# IMA Global Human Benefit Charter

Status: governance design anchored in repository; NOT yet a legally registered nonprofit entity.
Date: 2026-10-17

## Mission
IMA is intended to serve humanity as a global, intergenerational, human-centered intelligence layer: protecting human agency, dignity, privacy, provenance, learning, accessibility and continuity across generations, cultures, languages and technologies.

## Legal boundary
This document is an internal governance specification. It does not itself create an Israeli amuta, public-benefit company, foundation, charity, tax-exempt entity, or international legal person. Actual registration requires the appropriate jurisdiction, filings, governing documents, officers and legal/tax compliance.

Israel currently provides an official route for registering an association (עמותה) or public-benefit company (חל"צ). Israeli association rules include a prohibition on profit distribution to members and require assets/income to serve the association's purposes. Therefore IMA's nonprofit mission assets must remain legally separate from personal creator assets and inheritance rights.

## Public-benefit principles
1. No private ownership of IMA's humanitarian mission merely by virtue of founding it.
2. No distribution of nonprofit assets or profits to members.
3. No use of IMA as a vehicle for personal enrichment disguised as public benefit.
4. Transparent accounts, expenses, revenue, grants, donations and material conflicts of interest.
5. Human oversight, independent audit/oversight and documented decision rights.
6. Personal user memory remains user-controlled and is not an organizational asset merely because IMA stores or processes it.
7. IMA may pay legitimate compensation, license fees or creator royalties only through documented lawful contracts where permitted; this is not a distribution of nonprofit profit.
8. On dissolution, remaining nonprofit assets should transfer to another eligible public-benefit organization with compatible purposes, subject to applicable law.

## Relationship to Ori Cohen
Ori Cohen is recorded as Founder, Creator and Architect of IMA. Founder provenance is permanent and must not be erased by later governance, rebranding, technical evolution or succession.

The nonprofit mission and the creator's personal property are separate:
- IMA public-benefit assets: governed for humanity and the stated mission.
- Ori's personal intellectual property and contractual creator rights: remain personal property unless expressly assigned or licensed.
- Validly accrued creator compensation/royalties belong to Ori or his estate according to applicable contracts and law.
- No future operator may silently convert Ori's personal rights into nonprofit property.

## Global continuity
IMA may operate through different legal entities, countries, interfaces or successor organizations while preserving a public continuity record. A future legal entity must adopt this charter or a legally reviewed successor that preserves the same core principles.

## Financial architecture
Every financial event is classified as:
A. Income/revenue actually received or contractually receivable and verified.
B. Expense actually incurred/paid or contractually committed and verified.
C. Restricted funds with a defined legal purpose.
D. Creator compensation/licensing/royalty.
E. Nonprofit reinvestment.
F. Authorized humanitarian/community benefit.
G. Tax, compliance, reserve or transaction costs.

No money is moved solely because a ledger entry exists; payment authority remains human and legally authorized.

## Surplus rule
The canonical rule is: revenue is first reconciled against lawful taxes, refunds, chargebacks, transaction fees and necessary authorized operating costs; maintain a documented reserve appropriate to the legal entity and risk; then apply the approved creator-rights/license mechanism; remaining nonprofit surplus is reinvested into IMA's public-benefit mission or otherwise applied according to the governing documents and applicable law.

No fixed percentage is invented here because no prior percentage allocation was found in the repository or retrieved IMA history. Percentages may be added later as explicit founder/governance parameters and must not be silently changed.

## Human-benefit priorities
When allocating lawful nonprofit surplus, prioritize:
1. keeping IMA reliable, secure and accessible;
2. preserving human knowledge, provenance and intergenerational continuity;
3. accessibility, multilingual and underserved-user access;
4. safety, privacy and responsible research;
5. education, creativity and tools that increase human agency;
6. grants or direct programs for public benefit when legally and operationally feasible.

## Transparency
IMA should publish a human-readable annual financial summary once a legal entity exists and sufficient data exists, while keeping private donor, user and employee information protected.

## No automatic spending
The system may detect opportunities, calculate budgets and prepare recommendations. It must not autonomously spend funds, sign contracts, donate, transfer money or incur paid advertising costs without the required human authorization and payment controls.
