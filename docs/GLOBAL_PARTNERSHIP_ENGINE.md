# IMA Global Partnership Engine

## Two-track operating model

IMA advances on two parallel tracks:

1. **CONNECTED** — cooperate with relevant organizations across the Internet ecosystem.
2. **INDEPENDENT** — continue building, testing, improving and distributing IMA without requiring any external partner.

Neither track may block the other.

## Ecosystem coverage

- Internet service providers and mobile operators
- Fiber, cable, satellite, Wi-Fi and terrestrial infrastructure
- Backbone, transit and peering
- Internet Exchange Points and local interconnection ecosystems
- DNS, registries, root/DNS infrastructure and domain ecosystem
- CDN, edge, cloud and data centers
- Network hardware and infrastructure vendors
- Operating systems, browsers and application platforms
- AI, cybersecurity, privacy and identity
- Accessibility and assistive technology
- Education, health and family technology
- Universities, research institutions and NRENs
- Open-source communities
- Standards and technical organizations
- Regulators and governments
- Civil society and international organizations
- Businesses, creators and end users

## Partner lifecycle

`DISCOVERED -> QUALIFIED -> CONTACTED -> REPLIED -> MEETING -> PILOT -> VERIFIED -> EXPANDED`

Alternative terminal states:

`DECLINED`, `DORMANT`, `BLOCKED`

A partnership is never claimed before acceptance is verified.

## Independent track

For every important external dependency, IMA should maintain an independent fallback where technically and legally feasible.

The independent track should:
- preserve a usable public product;
- maintain evidence-backed testing;
- avoid vendor lock-in where practical;
- preserve user agency and privacy;
- keep provenance and creator/IP boundaries explicit;
- continue improvements even when outreach receives no response.

## Outreach rules

- Prefer official partnership/contact channels.
- No mass spam.
- One clear value proposition per organization.
- Start with a small, reversible and measurable pilot.
- Never request secrets, credentials or privileged access by email.
- Never claim deployment, partnership or impact without verification.
- Record evidence and status for every meaningful external engagement.

## First external route

Rimon was the first concrete ISP outreach. The next global route should prioritize organizations whose published mission and partnership model overlap with IMA's goals, then expand country-by-country and layer-by-layer.

The Internet Society explicitly describes the Internet as a global ecosystem of interconnected networks and organizations and provides a dedicated partnership route for mission-driven organizations. This makes it a natural ecosystem-level contact, while IMA remains independent if no partnership is formed.

## Success condition

IMA becomes **connected without becoming dependent**:
- more connections increase capability and reach;
- loss of any single connection does not stop IMA;
- successful pilots become reusable capabilities;
- all claims remain evidence-backed.
