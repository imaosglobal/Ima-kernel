# Ori Cohen — Life Path, Body, Fear, Creation & Intergenerational Learning

## Purpose

This document preserves the creator's life-path inquiry as part of IMA's provenance and learning history.

It is not a diagnosis, a medical record, or a claim that every later experience was caused by an early-life event. It is a structured record of the creator's own account, with uncertainty marked explicitly.

The purpose is intergenerational learning:

- preserve what happened without turning it into destiny;
- distinguish event, interpretation, hypothesis, and evidence;
- document how a person can move from survival and adaptation toward agency, creation, connection and renewal;
- let future people learn from one human path without being told that they must reproduce it;
- allow IMA to revisit the story as new evidence, memories and interpretations emerge.

## 1. Beginning of life — extreme prematurity

### First-person account

Ori reports that he was born in Israel around the middle of the sixth month of pregnancy, with a birth weight of less than one kilogram.

He reports spending approximately the first six months of life in an incubator.

He also reports undergoing surgery because a fetal blood vessel did not close after birth. He identifies this as the ductus arteriosus (ductus arteriosus / PDA), but the exact medical terminology, dates and operative details should be verified from historical medical records before being treated as medically established.

### Inquiry

The important question is not to invent a psychological explanation from this history.

The documented question is:

**What does it mean to begin life in extreme medical vulnerability, and how might years of adaptation to the body and to institutions shape later experience?**

IMA must keep this as an open inquiry rather than a deterministic claim.

---

## 2. Childhood — repeated medical adaptation

Ori reports later undergoing:

- ear tube surgery ("כפתורים");
- eye surgeries related to strabismus;
- early education in a kindergarten serving blind and visually impaired children.

He reports that, through substantial work and adaptation, he later integrated into mainstream schools.

### Working interpretation

A recurring pattern appears:

**difference / difficulty → intervention → effort → adaptation → integration**

This pattern should not be romanticized. Difficulty is not automatically beneficial, and adaptation does not prove that suffering was necessary.

At the same time, the pattern may help explain a later recurring strength:

**the ability to enter a difficult system, learn its rules, adapt, and keep looking for another way to participate.**

This is a hypothesis to test against the fuller life history.

---

## 3. From survival to participation

The childhood story should not be preserved only as a story of problems.

It also contains evidence of agency:

- remaining engaged through repeated interventions;
- learning within specialized educational settings;
- moving into ordinary educational settings;
- continuing to develop capacities rather than accepting early limitations as the complete definition of self.

The intergenerational lesson is therefore not:

> "If you suffer, you will become stronger."

It is:

> **"A person's beginning does not have to dictate the full range of their future participation."**

---

## 4. Later life — a second layer of injury and disruption

Ori's later account includes significant physical injury and prolonged consequences for work, mobility, family visits, finances and his relationship with public systems.

A particularly important element of the current inquiry is the experience of being injured in a bus accident and subsequently developing fear around buses and travel.

This creates a distinction IMA must preserve:

**the original external event is not the same thing as the later fear response.**

The event deserves factual and legal documentation.

The fear deserves compassionate investigation.

Neither should be used to erase the other.

---

## 5. The present question — when fear becomes a boundary around life

Ori describes feeling as though fear has begun to manage parts of his life: avoiding buses, difficulty visiting family, reduced mobility, pain, and prolonged absence from work.

The current goal is not to declare fear irrational or to demand immediate exposure.

The working question is:

> **How can fear become information rather than the sole decision-maker?**

A future IMA-supported inquiry should distinguish:

1. actual present danger;
2. remembered danger;
3. bodily alarm;
4. avoidance;
5. chosen caution;
6. gradual expansion of freedom.

The goal is not "never feel fear."

The goal is:

**to increase the person's ability to choose while fear is present, without ignoring legitimate danger or medical limits.**

## 6. Responsibility without blame

The creator's question from 2026-10-04:

**"מה זה אומר לקחת אחריות על עצמי?"**

The working answer developed in the continuing inquiry is:

> **Taking responsibility for oneself means recognizing what happened and what cannot be changed, distinguishing it from what remains within one's influence, and taking ownership of present choices and actions without converting responsibility into self-blame.**

Therefore:

- responsibility is not guilt;
- responsibility is not denial of injury;
- responsibility is not pretending the past has no effects;
- responsibility is not forced productivity;
- responsibility includes asking for help, resting, setting boundaries and changing direction when those are the appropriate actions.

## 7. Renewal

The creator later proposed:

**"לעשות את מה שנדרש ממני בלי להתבכיין אולי. בלי להתעסק בעבר או לתת לו להשפיע עליי. להתחדש?"**

IMA's refined formulation is:

> **Renewal does not require deleting the past. It means refusing to let the past alone determine the future, while learning from it and acting in the present.**

The word "complaining" must not become a mechanism of self-silencing.

Saying "this hurts" can be truthful information.

The distinction is between:

**describing pain** and **allowing pain to become the only possible identity or action.**

## 8. The "bubble" hypothesis

Ori has described his experience as feeling like being inside a separate bubble or incubator.

This image connects several periods of his life:

- literal incubation at the beginning of life;
- specialized childhood settings;
- repeated medical intervention;
- later injury;
- fear and restricted mobility;
- prolonged interaction with institutions;
- a desire to return to the wider world without pretending that vulnerability never existed.

This is a powerful autobiographical metaphor, but IMA must label it as a metaphor, not as a proven psychological mechanism.

### Working hypothesis

Perhaps part of the current work is not simply "escaping the bubble."

It may be:

> **learning that the world can contain both vulnerability and participation, and gradually increasing the range of places, people, activities and experiences in which the creator can participate by choice.**

## 10. What future generations may learn

IMA should not teach future people:

"Be like Ori."

It should teach:

"Here is one documented human path. Examine it."

Possible lessons include:

### A. A beginning is not a destiny

A person may begin life under severe constraints and still develop new forms of participation.

### B. Adaptation is not the same as surrender

Learning how to function within a system can be a form of agency. It does not mean accepting every limitation forever.

### C. Pain should be taken seriously without becoming identity

Acknowledge the body. Investigate the pain. Seek evidence and appropriate help. Do not automatically reduce the person to the pain.

### D. Fear contains information but should not automatically become authority

Fear can signal danger, memory, uncertainty or learned alarm. It should be investigated rather than blindly obeyed or blindly defeated.

### E. Responsibility and compassion belong together

A person can say:

"I am responsible for what I do next"

without saying:

"Everything that happened to me was my fault."

### F. Renewal can be incremental

A new life does not necessarily begin with one dramatic transformation.

It can begin with one new action, one relationship, one journey, one song, one question or one experience that contradicts an old conclusion.

### G. Creation can preserve learning

A poem, song, research paper, system, conversation or technological artifact can carry experience beyond the lifetime of its creator.

But preservation must retain provenance and uncertainty.
