# IMA Continuous Learning Loop

IMA now has a canonical learning layer for turning external research into bounded product learning.

## Loop

1. **Observe** — collect evidence from the world, users, projects, failures and successes.
2. **Provenance** — retain source, timestamp and truth-gate state.
3. **Compare** — extract reusable patterns rather than copy products.
4. **Synthesize** — group learning across trust, agency, usefulness, adoption, accessibility, community, safety, evaluation and other domains.
5. **Propose** — generate candidate improvements.
6. **Authorize** — consequential product changes still pass the Human Agency Gate.
7. **Verify** — test the resulting behavior and record evidence.
8. **Repeat** — feed new evidence back into the loop.

## Initial external learning

The seed lessons are derived from current research on:
- human agency in AI education
- real-world AI deployment
- adoption/sustain/optimization
- global educational distribution
- community contribution
- conditional trust and human oversight
- continuous evaluation

The initial research is deliberately diverse: successful systems, adoption studies, governance research, and failure/constraint evidence. IMA should learn from what works **and** what does not.

## Non-negotiable boundary

Learning is not permission.

IMA may discover, compare, propose and test reversible low-risk changes. It must not silently publish, spend money, contact people, change legal status, or make other consequential changes merely because research suggests doing so.

## Next expansion

The same loop should progressively ingest:
- IMA user feedback and runtime failures
- accessibility testing
- multilingual and cross-cultural evidence
- local and global competitor/project patterns
- failed launches and abandoned products
- scientific and philosophical research
- economic sustainability evidence
- child-safety and high-stakes domain evidence
- deployment telemetry and controlled experiments

The goal is not to make IMA imitate successful systems. The goal is to make IMA **better at becoming IMA**: useful, truthful, humane, accessible, agency-preserving, testable and continuously improving.
