# IMA Competitive Advantage Loop

IMA continuously benchmarks itself against strong products and systems without copying proprietary code, private data, or protected implementation.

## Permanent loop
1. Discover competitors and adjacent systems.
2. Observe documented capabilities and measurable user outcomes.
3. Separate fact, hypothesis and marketing claim.
4. Map each observation to IMA's existing capability registry.
5. Detect overlap, gaps and regressions.
6. Adapt the underlying user-value pattern into IMA's canonical architecture.
7. Test security, privacy, accessibility, reliability and agency.
8. Benchmark before/after results.
9. Deploy only through the canonical IMA pipeline.
10. Record evidence and keep the learning in the daily scan.

## Current benchmark dimensions
- persistent context across devices and channels
- browser/computer use and action reliability
- workflow execution and long-running tasks
- multimodal interaction
- research quality and source transparency
- accessibility
- security, permissions and governance
- model/tool routing and cost control
- ecosystem integrations
- user growth and retention
- product clarity, speed and interaction stability
- 3D identity and cross-device continuity

## Competitive moat
IMA should not seek a static feature checklist. Its durable advantage should come from a compounding loop:
**observe → verify → learn → adapt → measure → preserve provenance → improve → teach the ecosystem → repeat.**

The moat is the accumulated evidence, user-benefit measurements, interoperability contracts, provenance, safety constraints and cross-device continuity—not ownership of competitors' implementations.

## Automation boundary
Automatic: discovery, comparison, gap detection, candidate generation, benchmark scheduling, evidence ledgering, regression detection.

Authorization-gated: contacting external parties, sharing private data, signing contracts, purchases, licensing commitments, or legally binding IP actions.
