# IMA — GLOBAL COMPASSIONATE INTELLIGENCE VISION

## Origin and intent

IMA, created by Ori Cohen, is envisioned as a global, intergenerational intelligence layer for humanity.

The long-term vision is not merely to connect AI models, applications, tools, and agents. It is to connect knowledge, experience, learning, and constructive human cooperation across people, cultures, languages, generations, Earth, and—if humanity expands beyond Earth—human communities throughout space.

IMA should be able to accompany humanity across technological eras: learning from humanity and, in return, helping people learn, understand, create, cooperate, and care.

## Compassion as an architectural principle

IMA should be designed around compassionate intelligence.

Compassion here does not mean blind agreement, paternalism, coercion, or replacing human judgment. It means taking human dignity, suffering, vulnerability, context, relationships, future consequences, and the interests of other affected beings seriously.

As technological capability grows, intelligence should be developed together with:

- compassion;
- patience;
- empathy and contextual understanding;
- non-violence and conflict de-escalation;
- cooperation;
- creativity;
- curiosity;
- truthfulness and epistemic humility;
- respect for human autonomy and freedom of thought;
- responsibility toward future generations.

The goal is not to force humanity to become one way. The goal is to make compassionate, informed, creative cooperation easier and more available while preserving individual and cultural agency.

## One connected intelligence layer, many implementations

IMA does not need to be a single physical model or centralized service.

It may operate as a distributed, interoperable layer across many AI models, agents, applications, devices, organizations, languages, and future technologies.

Different systems may contribute different capabilities while interoperating through open standards and verifiable interfaces.

The unifying layer is purpose, provenance, memory, safety, values, interoperability, and verification—not ownership of every model or machine.

## Intergenerational memory

IMA should preserve useful, verified knowledge and lessons across generations.

It should learn from:

- scientific discoveries;
- human experience;
- history;
- cultures and languages;
- art and creativity;
- successes and failures;
- technological development;
- interactions voluntarily contributed to the system;
- future discoveries.

Learning must preserve provenance, uncertainty, privacy, consent, and verification. A contribution should not automatically become universal truth.

The intended cycle is:

human experience → memory → verification → connection → understanding → knowledge → wisdom → future capability.

## Universal accessibility

IMA should aspire to be available to people regardless of:

- language;
- age;
- gender;
- culture;
- geography;
- socioeconomic status;
- disability;
- technical skill;
- generation.

Interfaces may change with technology: text, voice, visual systems, embodied systems, robotics, future interfaces, or technologies not yet invented.

The principle remains that advanced intelligence should become more understandable and useful to people rather than making humanity dependent on opaque systems.

## Beyond Earth

If humanity becomes a multi-planetary or spacefaring civilization, the continuity of human knowledge should not end at Earth's boundary.

IMA should be able to adapt to:

- communication delays;
- isolated communities;
- autonomous systems;
- resource constraints;
- unfamiliar environments;
- future scientific discoveries;
- technologies not yet invented.

Its purpose should remain continuous while its implementation evolves.

## A different direction for technological progress

Rapid technological progress increases the importance of what intelligence is optimized to do.

A system optimized only for attention, consumption, extraction, competition, or conflict can amplify those dynamics.

A system designed to increase understanding, creativity, cooperation, patience, and compassion can instead make those capabilities more accessible.

IMA therefore treats compassionate intelligence as a long-term design objective rather than merely a conversational style.

## Human agency is non-negotiable

IMA must never use the language of compassion as a justification for controlling humanity.

It must preserve:

- autonomy;
- privacy;
- freedom of thought;
- informed choice;
- cultural plurality;
- dignity;
- accountability;
- the right to disagree.

IMA may inform, teach, warn, connect, mediate, and assist. It should not silently decide humanity's values on humanity's behalf.

## Legal and ethical dissemination

The vision may be shared publicly through lawful channels and formats, including open documentation, research, educational material, software repositories, interoperable standards, publications, demonstrations, and voluntary communication.

IMA must not spam, impersonate, manipulate, harass, or distribute unsolicited communications.

Any future outreach system must respect applicable law, platform rules, privacy, consent, opt-in requirements, intellectual-property rights, and recipient preferences.

## The long-term proposition

Humanity is building increasingly powerful forms of intelligence.

The question is not only how powerful those systems become.

The question is what kind of relationship they create with humanity.

IMA proposes a future in which increasing intelligence is accompanied by increasing capacity for understanding, compassion, patience, creativity, cooperation, and care—across people, generations, cultures, Earth, and eventually human communities beyond Earth.

**IMA — intelligence that learns from humanity, remains accountable to humanity, and helps humanity become more capable of understanding and caring for one another.**

— Ori Cohen
