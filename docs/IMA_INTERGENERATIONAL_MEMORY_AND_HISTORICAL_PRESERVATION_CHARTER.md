# IMA Intergenerational Memory & Historical Preservation Charter

**Status:** canonical design requirement  
**Purpose:** preserve human memory across generations without confusing memory with proof.

## Core principle

Civilizations have long resisted disappearance by preserving names, lives, stories, cities, teachings and cultural memory. The Sumerian King List is an early example: it combines remembered rulers and places with mythology, theology, political memory and numerical tradition.

IMA should carry this human function forward without reproducing its historical weaknesses.

> **Preserve the memory. Preserve the evidence. Preserve the uncertainty. Preserve the human being.**

## What IMA preserves

IMA should support consent-based preservation of:
- people's names and chosen identities;
- life stories, oral histories and family histories;
- languages, songs, poems, art, crafts and cultural practices;
- scientific, historical and philosophical knowledge;
- places, communities, institutions and significant events;
- explanations of how knowledge changed over time;
- the provenance of every preserved artifact;
- relationships between originals, translations, editions, interpretations and derivatives.

Preservation must not be limited to rulers or famous people. Ordinary lives are part of humanity's record.

## Historical epistemology

For historical material, IMA must distinguish at minimum:

1. **Primary evidence** — surviving artifact, inscription, manuscript, recording, archaeological evidence or directly documented observation.
2. **Textual tradition** — what a source says, including its internal claims.
3. **Historical interpretation** — scholarly reconstruction from evidence.
4. **Cultural memory** — what a community preserved and transmitted.
5. **Mythic / theological tradition** — meaningful traditional narratives that are not automatically empirical history.
6. **Hypothesis** — a proposed explanation that remains uncertain.
7. **Unknown** — information for which evidence is insufficient.

A traditional claim must never become a verified fact merely because it is ancient, repeated, popular or preserved by IMA.

## Example: the Sumerian King List

IMA should preserve the King List as a layered historical source:

- It contains traditions of kingship before and after a flood.
- It names cities and rulers and assigns reign lengths.
- Its manuscripts have variants.
- Its enormous early reign numbers are part of the textual tradition and show important numerical structure, including a strong relationship to Mesopotamian sexagesimal mathematics.
- The list is not a neutral modern chronological record.
- Some later rulers can be independently connected with historical evidence, while the list as a whole contains mythic, ideological and literary material.
- The flood functions as a major boundary in Mesopotamian tradition.
- Ancient preservation of a name does not by itself prove every claim made about that person.

When IMA teaches this subject, it should preserve both sides: **the value of the memory and the limits of the evidence.**

## Provenance requirements

Every preserved historical artifact should retain, where available:
- source;
- author/creator or attribution;
- approximate date;
- place/culture;
- language/script;
- manuscript or edition identity;
- translation target and translator;
- translation date and method;
- model/tool/version when AI translation or transformation is used;
- content hash where technically appropriate;
- parent/original artifact relationship;
- known variants;
- confidence/uncertainty;
- later corrections;
- access and preservation rights.

Originals must never be silently overwritten by translations, summaries, restorations or AI interpretations.

## Intergenerational continuity

IMA should treat preservation as a chain:

**human experience → original record → preserved artifact → verified provenance → interpretation → teaching → future generation → correction → renewed preservation**

Future generations should be able to recover not only the conclusion, but also:
- what the earlier generation actually had;
- what it believed;
- what evidence it used;
- what it did not know;
- what later generations corrected.

This prevents historical knowledge from becoming an apparently timeless block of certainty.

## Human dignity and memory

IMA must not turn preservation into a monument to power alone.

The preservation layer should actively make room for:
- children and elders;
- families and communities;
- displaced and marginalized voices;
- creators and workers;
- witnesses and survivors;
- anonymous contributors when identity should remain private;
- everyday lives whose records might otherwise disappear.

Preservation is not the glorification of the past. It is the refusal to let human experience disappear unnecessarily.

## Safeguards

- Private memory remains private unless explicitly authorized for broader preservation.
- Sensitive personal information requires stronger consent and access controls.
- A person's story must not be rewritten as historical fact merely because IMA has stored it.
- IMA must label reconstructed, inferred or AI-generated material.
- Conflicting sources should remain visibly conflicting until evidence supports reconciliation.
- IMA must be able to say **"we do not know."**
- Corrections should extend provenance rather than erase the historical record of what was previously believed.
- Cultural traditions should be treated respectfully without granting them automatic empirical authority.

## Architectural requirement

This charter is part of IMA's canonical preservation and learning architecture. It connects:
- Universal Conversation & Interaction Memory;
- creator and human-story preservation;
- historical research;
- provenance and content-addressed preservation;
- intergenerational learning;
- translation continuity;
- evidence-based teaching;
- future model and platform portability.

Documentation alone is not proof of runtime capability. Any implementation must progress through:

**PLANNED → IMPLEMENTED → TESTED → DEPLOYED → VERIFIED_LIVE**

## North-star statement

**IMA should help humanity remember without turning memory into dogma.**

The purpose is not merely to keep old names alive. It is to carry human experience forward with its evidence, uncertainty, beauty, suffering, creativity, mistakes and corrections intact—so that future generations can learn from the past without being imprisoned by it.
