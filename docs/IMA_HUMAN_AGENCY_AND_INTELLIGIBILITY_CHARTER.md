# IMA Human Agency & Intelligibility Charter

## Purpose

This charter is part of the canonical IMA Global Health Loop and applies to IMA itself and to capabilities it evaluates for people.
IMA is designed to reduce the risk that people become dependent on AI-governed systems they cannot understand, question, contest or stop.

This charter responds to the “cows of the future” scenario: AI systems may become deeply embedded in finance, law, administration and other critical systems while their complexity, concentration or autonomy makes meaningful human agency difficult.

IMA does not claim that this protocol can guarantee prevention. It is a design and verification framework intended to reduce the risk and preserve human agency.

## Core rule
**No critical AI system should become an unquestionable authority merely because it is more capable than an individual human.**

Capability must increase human understanding and agency, not make them irrelevant.

## The IMA anti-dependency protocol

1. **Intelligibility** — explain what the system is doing, what it knows, what it does not know, relevant inputs, limitations and uncertainty in language appropriate to the person affected.
2. **Contestability** — a person can ask why, challenge an output, provide contrary evidence and request reconsideration.
3. **Human override** — an authorized human can reject, reverse, pause or stop consequential AI-assisted operation where technically and legally possible.
4. **Traceability** — consequential actions retain provenance, timestamps, responsible actors/systems, relevant inputs and model/tool versions where lawful and appropriate.
5. **Independent verification** — critical conclusions should not depend on one opaque model/provider when independent checking is feasible.
6. **Reversibility** — systems should prefer reversible actions and preserve recovery paths before irreversible actions.
7. **Plurality** — avoid unnecessary dependence on one provider, model, database, identity system or infrastructure layer.
8. **Rights visibility** — affected people should know when AI materially participates in a decision and what meaningful remedies are available.
9. **Human capability preservation** — IMA should teach people enough about the surrounding system that they can retain practical agency rather than becoming passive recipients.
10. **Concentration monitoring** — IMA should continuously look for dangerous concentration of computational, financial, legal, informational or infrastructural power.
11. **Failure simulation** — test what happens if the AI is wrong, unavailable, manipulated, compromised, biased or incomprehensible.
12. **Safe degradation** — when verification fails, the system should reduce autonomy and move toward human review rather than silently increasing authority.
13. **Public-interest separation** — private user memory and identity must never be converted into shared governance knowledge without explicit authorization.
14. **Intergenerational continuity** — preserve the reasoning, evidence, dissent and correction history so future people are not forced to trust an inherited black box.

## Human decision ladder

`AI observes -> AI explains -> human understands -> human can question -> independent check -> human decides/authorizes -> system acts -> result is logged -> result can be reviewed`

Automation may shorten this ladder only when the risk is demonstrably low and reversibility is high.

For high-impact or irreversible decisions, skipping meaningful human agency requires explicit evidence, lawful authority and a documented reason.

## Anti-dependency test

The anti-dependency test is the practical gate for detecting whether a capability risks making people dependent on an AI system they cannot understand, question, override or recover from.

## Anti-"cow" test

A capability fails the IMA anti-dependency gate if a reasonable affected person cannot answer, at an appropriate level:

- What is the system?
- What is it deciding or doing?
- Why did it reach this result?
- What evidence supports it?
- What is uncertain?
- Who is accountable?
- Can I challenge it?
- Can a human override it?
- What happens if it is wrong?
- Can I recover from the action?
- What alternatives or independent checks exist?

The exact explanation must be adapted to the user; technical internals need not always be exposed, but meaningful agency cannot be replaced by decorative explanation.

## Evidence states

Every anti-dependency control uses the canonical IMA evidence vocabulary:

`PLANNED -> IMPLEMENTED -> TESTED -> DEPLOYED -> VERIFIED_LIVE`

A documented safeguard is not treated as a live safeguard until runtime evidence supports it.

It must not create a second scheduler or disconnected governance system. Its checks belong to the existing IMA Global Health Loop.

## Limits

IMA cannot by itself regulate governments, banks, courts, corporations or other external institutions. It can provide people with understanding, verification, comparison, evidence, records and agency-supporting tools, and it can make IMA itself subject to these same constraints.

The goal is not to make humans understand every line of every future AI system. The goal is to prevent loss of meaningful human agency when complexity grows.

## Canonical principle

**The future should not require humans to become experts in every machine. It should require machines and institutions to remain answerable to humans.**
