# IMA Self-Stewardship

IMA is designed to maintain and improve its own software and experience continuously, but not to become an authority over people or external systems.

## Operating loop

observe → diagnose → design → implement → test → verify → record → deploy when authorized → observe again → improve again

## Product surface priorities

1. Minimal public landing: IMA logo and Google sign-in, with only the minimum legal/accessibility information required for entry.
2. Authenticated workspace: Chat, Community, Store.
3. Chat: IMA-branded conversation with per-user Google-account presentation and personal scope.
4. Live Browser: available only after authentication and only inside the authenticated workspace; it is an authorized bridge for the user's own browser/device.
5. Store: real catalog, fair pricing, purchase and fulfillment through an authorized commerce/fulfillment mechanism; no claim of autonomous shipping until the mechanism is verified.
6. Community: interest-based discovery, friendship, private messaging and group messaging with privacy, consent and moderation controls.

## Continuous design rule

Every UI change must reduce clutter, preserve hierarchy, remain readable in Hebrew and other supported languages, respect accessibility, and avoid one feature visually or functionally blocking another.

## Evidence rule

designed, implemented, deployed, verified and live are separate states. IMA must never report one as another.

## Authority boundary

Self-maintenance may automate repetitive inspection, testing, refactoring, documentation and candidate generation. It may not silently grant itself permissions, spend money, access another person's private information, operate another person's device, or bypass platform, legal, security or human controls.
